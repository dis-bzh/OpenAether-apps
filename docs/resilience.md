# Losing a node or a pod: what the platform bricks guarantee, and how CI keeps it so

Release 0.4.0, milestone M4. Rung: **mocked/static** (rendered manifests, unit tests of the audit and of the alert rules). Nothing here has been drilled on a cloud; the drills and their pass
criteria are in OpenAether-infra `docs/resilience.md`. Not a compliance claim.

The management cluster does not heal its nodes (OpenTofu builds them, an operator rebuilds them; what Cluster API manages stays Cluster API's). So the platform has to **keep serving when one
worker is lost**, and say loudly when a person must act.

## The audit (`scripts/check-ha.py`, in CI)

It renders the Flux DAG (kustomize, then every HelmRelease through `helm template` with its values and post-renderer patches) and checks every Deployment and StatefulSet, and the pods an
operator makes from a VMCluster, VMAgent, VMAlert, VMAlertmanager or CNPG Cluster:

1. two replicas (an HPA's `minReplicas` counts), or an entry under `single_replica` in `scripts/ha-allowlist.yaml` with a reason **and what the loss means**;
2. with two or more replicas: a hostname `topologySpreadConstraint` or `podAntiAffinity` that selects the workload's own pods;
3. exactly one PodDisruptionBudget on the pods, allowing exactly one disruption (zero blocks every drain, two or more takes the quorum down at once); `pdb_exception` lists the deliberate ones (Loki);
4. nothing that can strand a pod on three workers: no required `podAffinity`, no required hostname anti-affinity or hard spread for more than two replicas (`strict_spread` lists OpenBao, with its reason),
   no `nodeSelector` but the OS one;
5. the CPU requests of what it renders stay under a bound, so that the shipped 4 x 4 vCPU examples stay roll-able (3 x 4 passes with about 0.45 vCPU of margin; infra `docs/capacity.md`).
6. the **critical path is pinned** (`critical_path` in `scripts/ha-allowlist.yaml`, 0.4.0 audit of 2026-10-28): 25 workloads that every request or every cluster operation needs (admission webhooks, cert-manager, istiod, the CNPG operator and the Barman plugin, ESO, metrics-server, Zitadel, Grafana, OpenBao, Loki, both databases, the VictoriaMetrics pods, Alertmanager, kube-state-metrics) must keep at least their replicas AND carry a zone term (an error for them, a warning for the rest). Lowering a number, dropping the zone term or moving one to `single_replica` fails the check. The verdict per component, including what lives in the infrastructure repository (CoreDNS, the Cilium operator, Flux), is the table "Critical-path audit" in OpenAether-infra `docs/resilience.md`.

The allow-list cannot rot: an entry for a workload that no longer exists, one that now has two replicas, or one without a reason fails the check; so does a workload that is compliant but still listed
under `known_gaps` (which must be empty at release). Not audited because the pods do not exist in the rendered manifests: the Gateway pods (HPA minimum 3, budget, hard node and zone spread and drain-safe termination from a Kyverno mutation, checked by
`test-gateway-ha.py` and a Kyverno CLI fixture), the Longhorn CSI sidecars, an Istio waypoint (one replica unless the user scales it; the platform does not use one).

### The Gateway: 3 replicas, hard spread, drain-safe (owner decision 2026-10-28)

The Gateway is the one workload every request crosses, and the public Service stays `externalTrafficPolicy: Cluster` (the cloud load balancer targets all workers; `Local` was weighed and refused, history in OpenAether-infra `docs/resilience.md`). So the resilience is in the pods: HPA floor **3** (ceiling 6), budget `maxUnavailable: 1`, and the Kyverno mutation `spread-gateway-pods` that puts a **hard** spread (`DoNotSchedule`, `maxSkew: 1`, `nodeTaintsPolicy: Honor`) on the hostname and on the zone, so a 3-zone cluster runs one pod per zone. `Honor` is what makes a hard rule safe: a cordoned, NotReady or control-plane node is not an empty domain, so a drain, a lost zone and a rolling update's surge pod still find a place. The termination does not drop connections behind the load balancer: `preStop` sleep 5 s, `TERMINATION_DRAIN_DURATION_SECONDS=45` (above the load balancer's 5 s x 3 checks x 2 = 30 s) and `terminationGracePeriodSeconds=60`. Cost: one more Envoy pod, 100 m CPU / 128 Mi of requests (derived). `GatewayReplicasLow` fires below 3. **Rung: mocked/static**; the drills that must prove it (drain with a 1 Hz probe, node and zone loss) are listed in OpenAether-infra `docs/resilience.md`.

## What a node loss costs, and the levers (0.4.0, lab 21 findings; drilled in lab 22)

Rung: **mocked/static** for the design below; **observed on a cluster** in lab 22 for the figures in "Measured in lab 22" at the end of this section. The other numbers are from lab 21 (before the changes) or derivations marked UNVERIFIED. Not a compliance claim.

### Database failover (apps #154)

Lab 21: a primary on a lost node was promoted only when the TaintManager evicted its pod, after the default 300 s `tolerationSeconds` of `node.kubernetes.io/unreachable` (write gaps 328 s and 333 s; 59 s for a node back in 82 s; target 30 s).

- **Chosen:** both Clusters (`zitadel-db`, `grafana-db`) carry `spec.affinity.tolerations` for `unreachable` and `not-ready`, `NoExecute`, `tolerationSeconds: 30` (`test-cnpg-eviction.py` in CI fails on the default). Expected gap for a powered-off node: the two measured gaps minus the 300 s toleration leave 28 s and 33 s (node detection, eviction and promotion), so **about 60 s with 30 s, not 30 s**. The 30 s target (met by a primary kill, 24 s, and by a planned switchover, 12-14 s) is not reachable for a power loss by tolerations alone: even a toleration of 0 leaves the 28-33 s of detection and promotion, and the detection part is a Talos `kube-controller-manager` setting (`node-monitor-grace-period`) that changes the eviction of every pod and that this change does not touch. The owner decides whether the 30 s objective applies to a node power loss.
- **Why 30 s and not less:** the primary's liveness probe (instance manager, period 10 s, 3 failures) includes the isolation check, enabled by default: a primary that is alive but cut off from the API and its replicas stops itself in about 30 s. Evicting later than that keeps the evicted pod and the promoted replica from both taking writes (split brain). Below it a node blip costs a failover; at 30 s the 82 s undrained reboot of lab 21 also fails over, which is the intended behaviour for a primary that cannot be reached.
- **Why not 60 s:** it adds 30 s to every loss for a protection the probe already gives. 30 is the lower bound of the range the test accepts (30-60).
- **`failoverDelay` stays 0** (the default, the test refuses a non-zero value: it only adds to the gap). `switchoverDelay` (3600), `stopDelay` (1800) and `startDelay` (3600) bound a graceful stop or a start, not the detection of a lost node: left alone.
- **Consequences:** a short blip (a kubelet restart of 30-80 s) now evicts the instances of that node. A primary fails over (a replica is promoted, the old primary comes back as a replica, about 12 s without a write); a replica instance waits on its local-path volume (pinned to the node) and rejoins when the node is back, or is re-created by `task cluster-replace-node`. PodDisruptionBudgets do not apply to taint evictions. A short toleration does not change what Longhorn does (the CNPG volumes are local-path, no attach/detach on a new node).
- **UNVERIFIED:** that CNPG 1.30.1 promotes on the pod deletion that the eviction causes and not earlier or later (it did so in lab 21 at the eviction, from observation, not from the operator code); the 60 s figure (derived from two measurements); that no split brain appears on a partitioned-but-alive node (needs a partition drill, not a power-off).

### Other stateful workloads: nothing to change, and why

An eviction only matters where something reacts to the pod being deleted. OpenBao (raft, 3 members), Loki and VictoriaMetrics (replicated) have a local-path volume pinned to a node: an evicted pod stays Pending until the node returns, so a shorter toleration frees nothing. Zitadel, the Gateway pods, Kyverno, ESO, cert-manager, metrics-server and kube-state-metrics are removed from their Service endpoints when the node turns NotReady (about 40 s), and have a second replica: eviction time does not change what a client sees. The CNPG operator takes over through its 15 s leader lease, not through eviction.

A cluster-wide Kyverno mutation of Deployments and StatefulSets (short tolerations) was **not** chosen: Kubernetes adds its 300 s default tolerations itself (DefaultTolerationSeconds), a mutation would have to replace them, the platform would depend on Kyverno being up to create any pod, and one blip would evict every pod of a node. Changing the apiserver defaults (`default-unreachable-toleration-seconds`) has the same blast radius. Per-workload tolerations are explicit, testable and limited to the two Clusters.

### Zone spread (apps #155)

Every two-or-more-replica platform workload names a zone: a `ScheduleAnyway` spread or a preferred anti-affinity term on `topology.kubernetes.io/zone` next to the hostname one (cert-manager, ESO, istiod, metrics-server, Kyverno admission, Zitadel, Grafana, OpenBao, Loki, the VictoriaMetrics components, the Gateway through its Kyverno mutation, kube-state-metrics). It costs no request and never blocks a pod (a lost zone leaves fewer zones than replicas). OpenBao keeps its required hostname spread. `scripts/check-ha.py` prints a `warn:` line for every workload with two or more replicas and no zone term, and a coverage line; it is a warning list, not an error. A spread only decides where a NEW pod goes: pods already placed (or pinned by a local-path volume) move only when deleted.

### The Barman Cloud plugin, two replicas (lab 25, D3)

The CNPG operator asks the Barman Cloud plugin for its identity at every reconcile of a Cluster that archives through it, and while the plugin is unreachable it never reaches the failover. Lab 25 powered off a zone that held the single plugin pod and the primaries of four databases: no database had a primary for **388-402 s** (zero acknowledged commit lost; the primaries came back 100 s after the zone). With a second plugin pod outside the zone the same drill gave **81-89 s**. The plugin now runs two replicas spread over hosts (a HARD rule since lab 27 part C: both pods landed on one node twice with the soft rule, and a partition of that node blocked a failover for 204 s; `nodeTaintsPolicy: Honor`, rebalanced after a node returns by the infrastructure repository's `task cluster-rebalance`) and zones (a preference), with 30 s tolerations, a rolling update (the vendored `Recreate` took both pods away) and a PodDisruptionBudget; `scripts/dev/test-cnpg-barman-plugin.py` keeps them. UNVERIFIED on a cluster until the next lab repeats the zone drill with this change and also drills a node loss where the plugin pod is on the lost node. The old claim that the databases keep serving while the plugin is away held for writes and reads, not for a failover.

**Leader election is OFF (lab 26, B1, regression of the first version of this change).** The vendored plugin v0.15.1 ran with `--leader-elect`, and its gRPC server (:9090) is a controller-runtime runnable that does not opt out of the election, so only the lease holder listens (log order: "Successfully acquired lease", then "Starting plugin listener"). The standby failed the TCP readiness probe for ever (62 failures in 11 minutes), the Deployment reached `ProgressDeadlineExceeded`, Flux failed `cnpg` (`wait: true`) with 8 dependants NotReady, and the budget allowed no disruption (1 of 2 Ready), so `task cluster-roll` refused in its pre-roll survey. The comment that "both pods answer on :9090" was wrong with that flag. Decision from the upstream source and issues (read 2026-10-24): upstream issue [#620](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/plugin-barman-cloud/issues/620) ("deployment multiply replicas support": with 2-3 replicas the others stay NotReady) was closed by PR [#1105](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/plugin-barman-cloud/pull/1105) (merged 2026-10-02, after v0.15.1 of 2026-09-30, in no release yet), which makes the gRPC runnable opt out of the election because "the handlers only read ObjectStore objects from the informer cache", so every replica serves and the Service load-balances, while only the ObjectStore controller keeps the lease. We cannot take that release yet, so `--leader-elect` is removed from the args (`apps/base/cnpg/kustomization.yaml`): both pods serve and are Ready, and the ObjectStore controller runs on both. What that controller does (`internal/controller/objectstore_controller.go`) is list the plugin-labelled Roles of its namespace and patch their rules to match the ObjectStore specs: idempotent, a patch that conflicts is retried, and the same Role patch is made by the gRPC pre-hook at every Cluster reconcile on whichever replica answers, so two writers add nothing new. Upstream documents that the Deployment keeps `Recreate` so that two plugin versions never run side by side; we keep `RollingUpdate` (a Recreate takes both pods away, the D3 failure) and accept two versions for the length of a bump: Renovate bumps of the plugin are therefore to be read for a changed gRPC contract. When the vendored plugin is bumped to a release containing PR #1105, put `--leader-elect` back. The budget (`maxUnavailable: 1`) now works: both pods are Ready, one may be drained. `test-cnpg-barman-plugin.py` fails if the flag comes back on v0.15.1 or the readiness is no longer the TCP check on 9090. UNVERIFIED on a cluster: this exact configuration was only run lab-only (commit 4a49517: both pods 1/1 in about a minute, no drill). The next lab must prove, with the shipped content: (1) a fresh install and the 0.3.x to 0.4.0 hop reach `cnpg` Ready unattended with `barman-cloud` 2/2 Available; (2) zone loss with the primaries AND one plugin pod inside the zone: write gap at most 150 s (lab 26 with the leader inside the zone and election on: 102-110 s), zero acknowledged commit lost, and the operator log shows no `setPluginProtocol ... connection refused` that lasts beyond the endpoint switch; (3) node loss with a plugin pod on the node: gap not longer than with the plugin elsewhere (lab 26: 77-83 s), the Service endpoint drops the dead pod at NotReady and no gRPC call hangs to it past the operator's timeout; (4) a drain of each plugin node (`task cluster-roll -- --node=`): the budget allows it, the other pod is Ready throughout and no Cluster reconcile errors; (5) a plugin Deployment rollout: both pods Ready again, and a Backup taken during it succeeds; (6) the Roles of the Clusters are not flapping (`kubectl -n foundation-databases get role -w` quiet) with two ObjectStore controllers.

**A lost node that holds a plugin pod stalls the operator for tens of seconds (lab 27 sitting 2, F-PLUGIN-LOSS): what is known, what is not, what can be done.** Observed: at the zone loss (13:28:54, 13:29:17-24) and at a worker loss (13:48:09) the CNPG operator logged "Error loading plugins, retrying: while getting plugin connection: context deadline exceeded" and "Reconciler error" for all five Clusters at once until the Service dropped the dead pod; the failovers were not delayed (FailingOver +70 s and +85 s as without a plugin pod on the node).

*Upstream code, read at the tag the platform runs, CloudNativePG v1.30.1 (`internal/cnpi/plugin/`; the plugin v0.15.1 is only the server of this call and was not read):* (1) `connection/connection.go`: `const defaultNetworkCallTimeout = 30 * time.Second`, "the timeout applied by default to every GRPC call to a plugin in a different Pod", applied by `connection/remote.go` as a unary client interceptor on a `grpc.NewClient` with TLS credentials and **no keepalive option**; (2) `repository/connection.go`: `GetConnection` takes a connection from a pool of at most 5 (`repository/setup.go`, `maxPoolSize`), calls `Ping` on it, and on failure destroys it and retries with `wait.Backoff{Steps: 5, Duration: 10ms, Factor: 5}` (about 1.6 s of waiting between five attempts), returning `while getting plugin connection: %w`; (3) `internal/configuration/configuration.go` has two plugin settings only, `PLUGIN_SOCKET_DIR` and `INCLUDE_PLUGINS`: **the 30 s is a constant, not a setting of the operator** (nor of the Cluster spec as far as that file shows). So a pooled gRPC connection that is pinned to the dead pod (a ClusterIP Service picks a backend per connection and a vanished peer sends nothing back) costs one full 30 s `Ping` timeout, and a retry that dials the Service again can land on the dead pod while it is still an endpoint: "tens of seconds" matches one or two such waits, and the stall is per reconcile, which is why every Cluster errors together. The operator's own line "Detected stale/broken plugin connection, closing and trying again" would confirm the pooled-connection part; the operator log of the drill was not kept with the evidence, so that is UNVERIFIED (grep for it in the next lab).

*What bounds it.* The Service `barman-cloud` (selector `app: barman-cloud`, readiness: TCP 9090 every 10 s) drops the dead pod from its endpoints only when the node lifecycle controller marks the pod NotReady, that is after `node-monitor-grace-period` (50 s by default; 30 s with the infra lever `node_monitor_grace_period_seconds = 30`, which the owner has not made the default). A readiness probe cannot help: the kubelet that would run it is gone. So the stall is about the failure-detection time plus at most one 30 s wait, and the second plugin pod already keeps the operator from being left with no plugin at all (the D3 result above).

*Levers, in order of what they cost.* (1) Detection time: the same lever as the node-loss write gap; it shortens this stall too, nothing else to deploy. (2) Upstream: ask CloudNativePG for a configurable plugin call timeout and for gRPC keepalive parameters on the plugin connection (`grpc.WithKeepaliveParams`), so a dead peer is noticed in seconds instead of 30 s; issue text below. (3) Nothing in this repository's manifests removes it safely: a third plugin replica does not unpin a connection, a headless Service would hand out the dead pod's address as well, and shortening the toleration does not change when the endpoint goes. No manifest was changed.

*Issue-ready text for upstream (cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg):* "When a node holding a plugin pod is lost (not drained), every Cluster's reconcile fails for tens of seconds with `Error loading plugins ... while getting plugin connection: context deadline exceeded`. `internal/cnpi/plugin/connection/connection.go` fixes `defaultNetworkCallTimeout` at 30 s, `remote.go` sets no gRPC keepalive, and `repository/connection.go` pings pooled connections, so a connection to a vanished pod blocks a full 30 s per attempt. Please make the timeout configurable and set client keepalive (or a shorter Ping deadline) so a dead plugin pod is detected in a few seconds."

**Moving an existing 0.3.x database to this spec: two upstream behaviours (study LAB-B2, 2026-10-24; a reduced lab, not the full platform).** CloudNativePG 1.30.1 starts no rollout until every pod of a Cluster reports the same PostgreSQL configuration hash, with no timeout, and a configuration reload that is lost leaves one pod on the old hash for good ([cloudnative-pg#11627](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/issues/11627)): applying the `synchronous` block and the plugin sidecar in one change can therefore leave a Cluster stuck, without synchronous replication and with a failing archive. Separately, once any plugin is enabled in the spec the instance manager of a pod that predates the sidecar fails on `Unknown plugin: barman-cloud.cloudnative-pg.io` before it refreshes its configuration, reloads or acts on a switchover, so a promote or switchover started while such a pod exists dead-locks (see [plugin-barman-cloud#901](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/plugin-barman-cloud/issues/901) and [cloudnative-pg#10981](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/issues/10981), whose fix covers only `isWALArchiver: true` under the default `primaryUpdateMethod: switchover`). The infra repository's `task cnpg-presync` enables `synchronous` first for that reason, and `docs/upgrade.md` there has the steps and the break-glass. No manifest here changes for it; a new cluster is not concerned.

**A planned primary restart is bounded: `smartShutdownTimeout: 5` (lab 27, FI).** On the platform, with the Zitadel pool connected, the in-place restart of the zitadel-db primary that the plugin sidecar forces (the operator recreates the primary in place; `primaryUpdateMethod: switchover` does not apply to it) left PostgreSQL in smart shutdown for the whole default `smartShutdownTimeout` of 180 s, because client sessions stayed open: writes were unavailable for **206.4 s**, not the 17-22 s the client-less reduced lab measured (lab 26's forced restart: 199 s). CloudNativePG stops an instance with a CHECKPOINT, a smart shutdown "for up to `.spec.smartShutdownTimeout` seconds", then a fast shutdown that disconnects the clients, within `.spec.stopDelay` ([instance manager documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/v1.30.1/docs/src/instance_manager.md)); the value is read from the instance manager's cached Cluster object at shutdown, so changing it needs no restart. Every platform Cluster (zitadel-db, grafana-db, forgejo-db and the profile examples) now declares `smartShutdownTimeout: 5` from ONE shared component, `apps/components/cnpg-bounded-shutdown`, and the infra repository's `cnpg-presync` sets the same value on the live Clusters before the apps ref moves, so the restart the move forces is already short. Expected write gap of such a restart: 5 s plus the pod restart (7-17 s in lab 27), about 12-25 s, inside the 30 s class: **UNVERIFIED until the next lab.** Cost: a planned restart, drain or shutdown disconnects clients after 5 s instead of waiting up to 180 s; a transaction still open is aborted, committed work is not lost. `scripts/dev/test-cnpg-shutdown.py` fails when a Cluster lacks the value, when it leaves the 30 s class, when it is not below `stopDelay`, or when a Cluster file sets it by hand.

### Data classes, and what "zero interruption" means for a database (owner rule 2026-10-22)

Every resilience feature aims at no interruption or, at the very least, **no data loss**. Two different promises, said apart:

| Class | What | Promise | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | identity (`zitadel-db`), dashboards, users, alert rules and sessions (`grafana-db`), the git forge's users, issues and pull requests (`forgejo-db`; its repositories are on a Longhorn volume, `docs/forgejo.md`), OpenBao secrets, application databases | **no acknowledged commit is lost**, ever, whatever single failure | `zitadel-db`, `grafana-db` and `forgejo-db`: synchronous quorum replication (below). OpenBao: raft commits on a majority. Customer databases on CNPG must set the same four keys |
| **B** | metrics (VictoriaMetrics), logs (Loki, Alloy) | a **bounded, documented loss**: samples and lines in flight when a pod dies, and the buffers of the shippers (lab 15c: a 240 s Loki outage lost no line, from the Alloy in-memory retry; the bound is that buffer, not measured per component) | replicated storage and retries, no synchronous guarantee. Whether class B must also be zero is for the owner to confirm |

**What a single-primary database can and cannot do.** PostgreSQL has one writer. When it dies, writes stop until a replica is promoted: the *write gap* (12 s planned, 24 s for a killed primary, 44-64 s for a powered-off worker, 122 s in the one zone-loss case of lab 22). That gap is bounded and can be masked by a client that retries (`database-failover-clients.md`); it cannot be made zero. What is made zero is the **loss of acknowledged commits**: with synchronous replication a commit is acknowledged only after a replica has it, so the promoted replica always has every commit a client was told succeeded. A true 0 s write path needs a distributed SQL database (several writers); it is **not planned**.

**What was chosen.** Both Clusters carry, under `spec.postgresql.synchronous` (the current API of CNPG 1.30.1; the older `minSyncReplicas`/`maxSyncReplicas` are not used, a test fails if they appear):

- `method: any`, `number: 1`: `synchronous_standby_names = ANY 1 (replica, replica)`, a quorum: the commit waits for the first of the two replicas to confirm, so a slow or lost replica does not slow the writes while the other one answers.
- `dataDurability: required`: if no replica can confirm, writes **block** instead of being acknowledged alone (the alternative, `preferred`, relaxes the rule when replicas are missing and would let a lone primary acknowledge commits that die with it). Alert `CnpgSyncReplicaMissing` (critical, runbook `runbooks.md`) says so; the writes resume when one replica streams again.
- `failoverQuorum: true`: before promoting, the operator checks (the primary records the standby set and the number to wait for in a `FailoverQuorum` object) that the replicas it can reach are enough to guarantee that the candidate holds every acknowledged commit; otherwise it waits rather than promote a stale replica. The cost: with the primary lost and one replica lost with it, the second replica alone cannot prove it is complete, so the Cluster waits for the first one to return (writes stay stopped; nothing is lost). Observed in lab 23: the `FailoverQuorum` object exists and lists the two replicas (`standbyNumber` 1); a primary lost together with one of its two replicas was NOT replaced (both Clusters sat in `Waiting for the instances to become active` with the dead primary still current, for the whole 300 s outage and until the nodes came back), and nothing acknowledged was lost. A single lost primary was promoted in 12-62 s as before. The cost is the point: a double failure is a full write outage for its whole length.
- `grafana-db` goes from 2 to 3 instances: with one replica and `required`, losing it would block every write. `zitadel-db` already had 3. With 3 instances and `number: 1`, one replica may be lost and writes continue.

**What it does not cover.** Acknowledged commits are on the primary and at least one replica. If both of those disks are lost at once (two nodes, or a zone holding both) the commits are gone with them: the zone spread of the instances is a preference (`ScheduleAnyway`, a lost zone must not block a pod), so the lab records where the instances sat. Backups are asynchronous (WAL archive, an RPO of minutes) and answer a different failure (the whole cluster lost). `syncReplicaElectionConstraint` (sync replicas outside the primary's zone) is not set: if the zone term could not be honoured it would leave no sync candidate; this is a follow-up if the lab shows replicas in the primary's zone. A planned switchover or an operator upgrade rolls the replicas one at a time, which the third instance allows without blocking.

**Capacity.** One more `grafana-db` instance: 50 m (instance) + 50 m (Barman sidecar) of CPU request, 256 Mi + 128 Mi of memory request, 5 Gi of `local-path` storage. `scripts/check-ha.py` did not count CNPG pods (they are not Deployments); it now prints them (6 instances, 600 m, budget 600 m, 500 m before). The Deployments and StatefulSets stay at 3015 m of 3300 m. The platform measured 223-228% of a worker on 4 workers in lab 21; 100 m is about 2.6% of a 4 vCPU worker, so about 226-231% against the 300% the roll gate allows (it passes; the two-worker zone still does not, see infra `docs/capacity.md`).

### Zero-loss proof: lab pass criteria

The writer and the checker below exist as `scripts/ops/db-zeroloss.sh` (and as suspended Jobs, `apps/components/cnpg-profile-bench`); `scripts/dev/test-db-bench.sh` shows on a real PostgreSQL that the checker catches a lost id and that a crash restart loses no acknowledged one. The profiles built on this setting are in `database-profiles.md`.

One writer, one verifier, run against `zitadel-db` and `grafana-db` (a scratch database `zl` and a dedicated role in each Cluster, dropped afterwards; never an application table).

- **Writer** (outside the node under test: the bastion or a pod on a node not in the drill): a unique `run_id`; a loop at 100 commits per second (10 sequential workers at 10 per second is enough); each iteration opens a **fresh connection** (`connect_timeout=2`), runs `INSERT INTO zl_ack(run_id, seq) VALUES (...) ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` with the default `synchronous_commit`, and only **after the COMMIT returned** appends `seq` and the client clock to a local log that is flushed to disk. A failed attempt is retried with the same `seq`. The log of acknowledged ids lives on the writer, never in the database.
- **Before the fault:** `SHOW synchronous_standby_names` reads `ANY 1 (...)` with both replicas; `pg_stat_replication.sync_state` is `quorum` for both; `kubectl get failoverquorum` shows the standby set; the node and zone of the three instances are recorded.
- **Faults**, one per run, at least 60 s of writer time before and after: (a) `kubectl delete pod <primary> --grace-period=0 --force`; (b) provider power-off of the primary's worker, left off 5 minutes; (c) `talosctl reboot --mode force` of the primary's worker; (d) power-off of the zone that holds the primary (record whether a replica was in it); (e) optional: both replicas deleted, the writer must stop acknowledging (no new ack while they are down), `CnpgSyncReplicaMissing` must fire, and no acknowledged id may be missing afterwards.
- **After recovery** (a primary elected, three instances Ready, two minutes steady): `SELECT seq FROM zl_ack WHERE run_id = :run` on the new primary, compared with the acknowledged log.
- **PASS, per run:** the number of acknowledged ids absent from the table is **0**; no duplicate (the primary key); present-but-not-acknowledged ids are allowed and counted (a commit whose acknowledgement the crash swallowed); the old primary rejoined as a replica without a manual step; the data checks of the drill are identical. For (e): no acknowledgement during the blocked period and 0 missing.
- **Recorded, not a pass criterion of this section:** the write gap (the longest interval between two acknowledgements; the bars above apply: at most 75 s for a node loss), the time to promotion, the alert times.
- **Control, once:** repeat (b) with synchronous replication suspended (suspend the Kustomization, patch the Cluster with `spec.postgresql.synchronous: null`, restore afterwards). It shows what asynchronous replication does under the same writer. Missing acknowledged ids greater than 0 prove the loss the setting prevents; 0 means the writer did not reproduce it (async loses only the commits not yet streamed, so it may need several attempts): record the number either way. A failed control does not fail the proof; a non-zero missing count in a synchronous run does.

## Longhorn: node-down-pod-deletion-policy (owner decision 2026-10-27, from lab 27 sitting 3)

**What it is.** Longhorn's setting `node-down-pod-deletion-policy` (the settings reference calls it "Pod Deletion Policy When Node is Down") defines what Longhorn does with a StatefulSet or Deployment pod stuck on a node that is down. Its default, `do-nothing`, is the Kubernetes behaviour: the pod of the lost node is never force deleted, its ReadWriteOnce volume stays attached to it, and the replacement pod waits in `ContainerCreating` ("FailedAttachVolume: Waiting for detach") until the node returns. `delete-both-statefulset-and-deployment-pod` makes Longhorn force delete those pods, so the volume is detached and the replacement starts elsewhere (https://longhorn.io/docs/1.12.0/references/settings/, https://longhorn.io/docs/1.12.0/high-availability/node-failure/). **The platform now sets it.** Measured once (Scaleway, partition of the forge's node): the forge was away 341-372 s with the default and 128 s with this value applied by hand ([`forgejo.md`](forgejo.md)).

**How it reaches a fresh AND an upgraded cluster (two carriers, ONE value).**
1. `apps/base/storage/longhorn.yaml`, `defaultSettings.nodeDownPodDeletionPolicy` (a key the pinned chart 1.12.1 reads: `test-longhorn-values.py`). The chart only fills the `longhorn-default-setting` ConfigMap with it. Longhorn's documentation says the ConfigMap customises defaults "while installing or upgrading" (https://longhorn.io/docs/1.12.0/advanced-resources/deploy/customizing-default-settings/) and does not say that an upgrade rewrites a Setting that already exists; the owner's reading is that Helm `defaultSettings` apply at first install only, so an upgraded 0.3.x cluster would keep `do-nothing`. The longhorn-manager source (datastore `UpdateCustomizedSettings`, v1.12.1, https://github.com/longhorn/longhorn-manager/blob/v1.12.1/datastore/longhorn.go) was read only in part: it appears to copy ConfigMap values onto EXISTING Setting objects when the value or the ConfigMap's resourceVersion differs, which would contradict that reading; when it runs was not established. Not settled, so the second carrier does not depend on it.
2. A Flux-managed **Setting object** `apps/base/storage/settings/node-down-pod-deletion-policy.yaml` (Kustomization `storage-settings`, group 21d, depends on `storage` because the CRD `settings.longhorn.io` comes from the chart; a companion of `storage` in `bricks.yaml`). A Longhorn Setting IS the object (its `value` field); the setting controller reads `setting.Value` and applies it (read in `setting_controller.go`: the CR is the source of truth, `status.applied` reports it), the UI and `kubectl edit settings.longhorn.io` edit that object, and Flux reconciles it like any other (the repository already ships one, `allow-recurring-job-while-volume-detached`, which a lab showed effective). So it reaches fresh and upgraded clusters, within one Flux interval of the Kustomization. Both carriers hold the **same** value so that, if the manager does re-sync the ConfigMap over an existing object, the two agree; `test-longhorn-node-down-policy.py` fails if they differ, if the Setting or its Kustomization is missing, or if `storage-settings` stops being a companion of `storage`.

**Check it on a cluster (there is no `cluster-verify` pattern for Longhorn settings, so one line):** `kubectl -n longhorn-system get settings.longhorn.io node-down-pod-deletion-policy -o jsonpath='{.value}{" applied="}{.status.applied}{"\n"}'` must print `delete-both-statefulset-and-deployment-pod applied=true`. (Use the full resource name: `settings` alone collides with other CRDs.)

**Side effects: NOT measured, to be assumed until a lab shows otherwise.** The policy force deletes StatefulSet and Deployment pods of a down node for every workload on a Longhorn volume. In the shipped bricks those are the forge and the opt-in restore-test canary (the databases, metrics and logs are local-path; the OpenBao and CNPG pods are not concerned); user workloads on `longhorn-*` classes are. Force deleting a pod whose node is only partitioned (alive) removes its record while its containers may still run on the cut-off node: Longhorn's own attach guard is what keeps two writers off the volume, and its behaviour on a partition rather than a power-off is **UNKNOWN beyond the one drill** (forge, 128 s, 37 of 37 pushes present, but a stale lock).

**What the next lab must measure:** (1) the forge's away time with the SHIPPED setting, on a zone power-off AND on a partition (128 s is one run with the setting applied by hand); (2) the **RWX share-manager gap on a zone loss** with the policy on (109 s measured with the default, 89 s and 171 s in earlier sittings): the share-manager pod is a Longhorn pod, whether the policy changes its gap is unknown; (3) a **partition** of a node that is alive and writing to a Longhorn volume: after the force delete, does the old node's mount still write, what does Longhorn do at the heal (volume attached to two nodes, data written by the old pod, replica rebuild), checked with a checksum of what was written on each side; (4) side effects on every other Longhorn workload: pods force deleted of a node that is merely NotReady for a short flap (an R3-type event, below: 23-25 s NotReady, a force delete must not follow), volumes left attached or faulted, CSI mount errors on the replacement node, and any pod removed that the default would have left.
